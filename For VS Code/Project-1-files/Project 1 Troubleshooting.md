# Part 1 Assessing Publications: Troubleshooting

## Step 1

### Challenge
Publication is behind a pay wall and research administrator does not have institutional access to full publication.
### Solutions
* Attempt to assess relatedness using only publicly available publication abstract. This is likely to be feasible in only a small percentage of examples.
* Ask investigator to submit a PDF of the publication via email.
* Utilize institutional library to obtain the PDF of the publication. 
* Request PDF of publication via inter-library loan.

## Step 5
### Challenge 
The publication is in a different disease, injury, or condition than the grant funded by the organization, but the methods and/or analyses are the same used in the funded research.
### Possible Solution
The publication may still be considered Directly Related to the funded research if the ***intent*** of the grant was to fund the development of a novel research technique or analysis. The research administrator may be interested to follow how the use of this novel technique or analysis disseminates across research teams.

# Part 2 Recording Publications: Troubleshooting

## Step 3
### Challenge 1
The publication is a review article. Is there value in recording it in the repository?

### Solution
The answer depends on the types of portfolio evaluation questions the research administrator may wish to explore. If the review article explores unanswered questions in the field and the funded research is attempting to address those questions, then there could be value in evaluating the impact of the review article, seeing how many times it is cited by other publications and in what contexts. The research administrator can use their knowledge of the portfolio and its goals to decide whether the review, if recorded in the repository, should be labeled as Directly Related or Tangentially Related.

### Challenge 2 
Although the publication is Directly Related to the funded research, it is a pre-print rather than a peer-reviewed publication. Should it be recorded in the repository?

### Solution
Although they are not peer-reviewed, they are still publicly available and can be cited by other publications. Therefore, yes, pre-prints should be recorded in the repository. There are a number of reasons that investigators may choose to make their research available via a pre-print. Some possible explanations include (not all-inclusive list):
  * The results of the project are negative and would not otherwise be accepted for publication in a peer-reviewed journal. However, the investigator feels that there is value informing the field about what they attempted so that others do not spend precious time and money doing the same thing.
  * The investigator submitted the manuscript to a journal for peer review, but wanted to make the results available to the field in the the interim.
