# Background

A critical aspect of research administration is determining the impact that a funded research project (i.e., grant) has on its field. One means to accomplish this is for research administrators—referred to as administrators for simplicity—to track outputs that result from a funded research project. Published, peer-reviewed publications are one type of output that administrators track. Therefore, administrators must keep accurate and complete records of publications that result from the organization’s funded research.

However, researchers often do not accurately cite the grants that contributed to the research presented in the publication. To ensure meaningful impact analyses, administrators should only curate records of publications that they personally verify as accurately citing the grants they wish to evaluate.

# Defining Relatedness Criteria

| Relatedness Category | Definition|
| --- | --- |
| Directly Related | Some or all the data in the publication can be directly attributed to the funded grant, as confirmed through review of annual and/or final progress reports. The publication may contain any data from a funded grant; the entire publication does not have to be attributed to the grant. A Review Article may be considered directly related to the funded grant if the topic of the review is the same as the funded research. The publication not citing the grant does not preclude the publication from being categorized as Directly Related. |
| Tangentially Related | The work presented in the publication is downstream or in some other way related to the work funded by the grant but does not contain any original research/data funded by the grant. The publication may cite the grant, but review of the content demonstrates that there is no data in the publication that can be attributed to data or related experiments presented in any annual or final progress reports submitted for the grant. This relationship label is appropriate to use if the publication uses a resource (e.g., a database, experimental model, etc.) that was created with funding from the grant, but the studies using that resource were not funded by the grant. Make sure to use the Description/Notes box to indicate how the publication is tangentially related to the award. |
| Not Related | Publications that do not meet either the Directly Related or Tangentially Related criteria. Publications with no relationship to the grant should not be entered into the repository. | 

# Defining Publication Repository Terms

**Preprint**: A completed draft of a scientific document that has not been peer-reviewed, but is publicly available via an online repository such as bioRxiv. Additional information available from the National Library of Medicine.
 https://www.nlm.nih.gov/oet/ed/pmc/preprints/index.html.

**PMID**: PubMed IDs (PMID) are automatically assigned by PubMed after a manuscript is published in an indexed journal. All publications in PubMed have a PMID regardless of their type or funding source. 

**PMCID**: In 2005 PubMed Central (PMC) was designated as the repository for published papers submitted in accordance with the NIH’s Public Access Policy. An article receives a PMCID after it is deposited in PMC by either the journal or the publication’s author. Publications may not receive a PMCID if the Public Access Policy does not apply to the funding source(s) of the publication, the publication date is before 2008, or it has not yet been assigned because the publication was published within the last three months. Use of PMCIDs is beyond the scope of this documentation.

**DOI**: Digital Object Identifiers (DOI) are assigned to electronically published materials by the International DOI Foundation. DOIs are unique to each publication and will never change. Every DOI begins with “10”.
